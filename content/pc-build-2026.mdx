Choosing a CPU and GPU is the easy part of a build. Memory frequency, SSD interfaces, and PSU specs are where builds go wrong — this guide lays out the order that works.

## Memory: check the QVL before the frequency

DDR5's sweet spot is still around 6000MT/s: it pairs with EXPO on AMD boards, while Intel builds can try 7200MT/s for small gains. Check the motherboard's QVL list and confirm XMP/EXPO support before ordering — most "won't POST" disasters trace back to RAM the board never validated.

Beyond frequency, a stable 6000MT/s kit beats an unstable 7200MT/s one.
</ImageText>

## PCIe 5.0: the payoff is random I/O, not sequential

PCIe 5.0 SSDs pass 10,000MB/s sequential reads, but everyday launches are driven by random I/O, where PCIe 4.0 flagships still excel. Unless you copy huge files, skip the premium; if you buy one, confirm the second M.2 slot doesn't share lanes with the GPU.

## Power supplies: connectors before wattage

ATX 3.0/3.1 PSUs include the 12V-2x6 connector, which handles the power spikes that make older units shut down with new GPUs. Sizing is simple: 750W for mid-range, 850W for high-end — and an 80 Plus Gold unit with tight ripple control beats 1000W "headroom". The PSU protects everything else; don't skimp.

## The order that doesn't fail

Lock in your budget and use case, then verify in this order:

<Steps title="Build order">
<Step>Pick the CPU and confirm the socket and platform.</Step>
<Step>Verify motherboard compatibility and BIOS support.</Step>
<Step>Check memory against the QVL list.</Step>
<Step>Match PSU connectors (12V-2x6) to the GPU.</Step>
<Step>Plan case airflow before installing parts.</Step>
</Steps>

A compatibility checklist is worth more than any flagship part on the list — skip a step and you'll be troubleshooting for a weekend.
